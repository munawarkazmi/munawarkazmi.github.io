Try it

Eight of these projects run here, in your browser. Six are the project's own algorithm, ported line for line, and recompute the results committed in its repository; one serves the committed certified intervals and grades a score against them; one is a toy with the C++ core's costs. A test in this site's own repository checks each of the seven against its repository on every change. Start anywhere, every demo links to the next.

1 · Safety verifier

Draw a route. The verifier judges it.

Lay waypoints on a real occupancy map, or load what two language models proposed, and the same checks that gate Nav2 say safe or rejected, with the waypoint that failed.

Reproduces all 80 recorded verdicts, two models on six maps
2 · Navigation shield

Watch a bad route get repaired, or halted.

Verify the proposal, plan a recovery on the inflated grid, re-verify with both judges. Seal the goal and it halts rather than invent a path.

Reproduces 50 recorded decisions and 38 recovered paths waypoint for waypoint
3 · ESP32-CAM firmware

Wave at your camera. The firmware decides.

The shipped downsampler and detector, running on your frames in this tab: 192 block means, the cells that changed, the alert line. Nothing leaves the browser.

The exact pipeline from the repository, with a synthetic scene if there is no camera
4 · Plan checker

Edit a robot's plan and watch where it breaks.

Pick a trap, read what a model answered, then change the plan: skip a door, carry the knife into the nursery, and the checker names the step and the rule.

Reproduces all 1,350 recorded verdicts across four models
5 · Tool-call validator

Break a tool call. See which layer catches it.

Rename the tool, break the schema, point at an object that does not exist; the structural and the contract layer answer separately, as recorded.

Reproduces every recorded bucket and all 262 constructed cases
6 · Exact predicates

Move a point by one ulp. Float lies. Exact does not.

Adversarial cases from the committed corpus, with the floating-point and the exact predicate run side by side and the determinants shown.

Every corpus case: exact verdict as committed, float wrong where recorded
7 · Certified intervals

Type a planner's score. The band says what it means.

Thirty-two certified intervals by world and budget: inside the band, below a trajectory that exists, or above a ceiling that nothing can cross.

Served data equals the committed suite row for row, zero violations
8 · D* Lite against A*

Block the robot. Count what each planner redoes.

A wall lands in the route; D* Lite repairs the plan it has, A* starts from nothing, and the cell counts sit side by side. Both checked against Dijkstra.

A toy with the C++ core's exact integer costs; not a repository result

SubmittedIEEE RA-L, Aug 2026

Certified bounds on achievable legibility under a path cost budget

A benchmark measures what a planner achieved. This certifies what nothing could achieve: an upper bound on legibility over every admissible trajectory, not only the ones somebody tried. All 32 world and budget pairs hold with zero violations, and the narrowest gap anywhere is 0.0064.

Where it stops. The observer model is exactly reproducible and has never been validated against people, so these are bounds on a stated objective, not on what a human would infer.

plan-failure-bench: a machine-checkable benchmark of how language model planners fail

Every instruction either admits a valid plan or plants exactly one trap, and every ground-truth label carries a proof re-verified in CI. A deterministic checker scores each response, with no human or LLM judge. Across 18 runs on four models the failures are distinct and stable: Gemini 3.6 Flash survives full semantic obfuscation, while smaller models split between silent compliance and surface-anchored refusal.

Where it stops. Two symbolic PDDL environments and four models. Detection is never reported without its paired false positive count.

Enhancing human-robot companionship: verbal and non-verbal communication cues in humanoid robots

A full sim-to-real pipeline on the JD humanoid, from an Isaac Sim digital twin through ROS2 hardware integration, built so a gestural and a spoken condition could be delivered identically to 20 participants. Speech conveyed intent significantly more clearly than gesture, while engagement and warmth held comparable across both. The study was run during the MSc (2023-2024); the manuscript was written up for journal submission in 2025.

Where it stops. One deliberately mundane scenario, the robot asking to be recharged, with 20 participants. Every gestural failure was a failure of joint attention, which is why the design target is hybrid.

PilotIn active development

legible-motion-bench: do language models know when their motion is legible?

Measures legibility against path cost and constraint satisfaction, computed exactly, with no human rater and no model judge. Asked to plan in the same worlds, three language models called 116 of their 120 trajectories legible, including all 25 that were not physically possible.

Where it stops. Three models at one temperature is a pilot, not a finding. Next: whether the metric tracks what people actually perceive.

About

I work at the boundary between robotics research and shipped engineering. My academic work asks how humanoid robots should communicate so that people trust them appropriately: not too little, not too much. My applied work is the planners, verification layers and embedded firmware those robots run on.

I hold both to one standard: a stranger should be able to reproduce every number, and every project should say where it stops.

Corrections. Three projects have withdrawn earlier claims, and each page says so where the claim stood: field-trial accuracy figures on the ESP32-CAM page, hardware results on the safety verifier page, and a decay exponent and four constants on the blowup page. The repositories record what was withdrawn and why.
